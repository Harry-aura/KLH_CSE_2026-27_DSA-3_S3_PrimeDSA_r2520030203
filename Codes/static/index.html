The Miller–Rabin Primality Test Engine

The Miller–Rabin test is the cornerstone of modern computational number theory and public-key cryptography (RSA, Diffie-Hellman, ECDSA key generation). It provides rapid probabilistic primality testing for arbitrary-precision integers of any size.

⚡

1. Mathematical Foundation

By Fermat's Little Theorem, if $n$ is prime and $\gcd(a, n) = 1$, then $a^{n-1} \equiv 1 \pmod n$.

Miller–Rabin strengthens this by factoring $n - 1 = 2^s \cdot d$ with $d$ odd. For prime $n$, the sequence:

a^d, a^{2d}, a^{4d}, \dots, a^{2^s d} \pmod n

must start with $1$, or end with $n-1$ before reaching $1$, because the only square roots of $1$ modulo prime $p$ are $\pm 1$.

🛡️

2. Carmichael Numbers Resilience

Carmichael numbers (e.g. $561 = 3 \times 11 \times 17$) satisfy $a^{n-1} \equiv 1 \pmod n$ for all coprime $a$, completely breaking naive Fermat primality tests.

Miller–Rabin detects the presence of non-trivial square roots of $1$, ensuring at least $75\%$ of all possible bases $a \in [2, n-2]$ act as witnesses to compositeness for every composite number without exception.

🎯

3. Error Probability & Confidence

For any composite integer $n$, the probability that a randomly chosen base $a$ fails to detect compositeness is at most $\frac{1}{4}$.

After $k$ independent randomized rounds, the probability of false primality is:

P(\text{False Prime}) \le 4^{-k} = 2^{-2k}

For $k = 40$ rounds, $P(\text{error}) \le 2^{-80} \approx 8.27 \times 10^{-25}$, delivering cryptographic certainty $> 99.9999999999999999999999\%$.

⏱️

4. Time Complexity Analysis

Trial Division: $O(\sqrt{n}) = O(2^{b/2})$ (Exponential)
Miller–Rabin: $O(k \cdot \log^3 n) = O(k \cdot b^3)$ (Polynomial)
1024-Bit Trial Div: $\approx 10^{140}$ years (Infeasible)
1024-Bit Miller–Rabin: $< 5\text{ ms}$ (Real-Time)
